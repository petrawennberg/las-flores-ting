Open for Voting LFWC-2026-01

$3,000 Fire Recovery Fee Proposal

Special assessment of $3,000 per share to fund continued water operations and infrastructure repair following the Eaton Fire.

Author
Board of Directors
Introduced
January 22, 2026
Vote Deadline
March 16, 2026
Quorum Required
20% of shares

Summary

The Eaton Fire destroyed 75% of shareholder properties, cutting Las Flores’ revenue to a fraction of operating costs. Without additional funding, the company cannot sustain water service. The Board proposes a one-time assessment of $3,000 per share, payable as a lump sum or $50/month over 60 months, to bridge the gap until rebuilding restores normal revenue.

Specification

For
Approve the assessment

If approved, shareholders choose a payment option:

  • Lump sum: $3,000 per share (interest returned at end of 5-year term)
  • Monthly: $50/mo for 60 months, added to your water bill
Against
Reject the assessment

The Board would need to identify alternative funding sources to sustain operations.

Use of Funds

  • Ongoing system operations and maintenance
  • Emergency repairs to damaged mains and service lines
  • Reservoir restoration (two Lake Avenue reservoirs destroyed)
  • Employee salaries, utilities, and insurance
  • Bridging the revenue gap until rebuilding restores water sales

The assessment is temporary. It would only remain in place until revenues from water sales increase sufficiently to cover operating expenses. Shareholders with fire-damaged properties would not be assessed until they return to active service.

Motivation

Las Flores Water Co. is a mutual water company serving central and northern Altadena. The January 2025 Eaton Fire destroyed the residences or businesses of approximately 75% of shareholders, reducing active service connections from over 1,400 to roughly 575.

The company had built cash reserves sufficient for 6–9 months of emergency operations, but these are now depleting. Under the current rate structure, the remaining active accounts cannot generate enough revenue to sustain operations long-term.

Additionally, two reservoirs at the top of Lake Avenue were destroyed, and the company is currently relying on water flowing from Lincoln Avenue Water Company’s Coulter Reservoir. The company is working with FEMA, Cal OES, and the SBA to obtain rebuilding funds, and a $4 million state earmark has been established for Altadena fire rebuild activities.
